The rule book

The rules
  • Five a day, max. More than five and you start bargaining with yourself every evening.
  • Everything is daily, weekly, or monthly. Cheap little things are daily. Things that need a real time slot are weekly. Things you keep postponing are monthly.
  • If something keeps getting missed, it's in the wrong spot. Not a willpower thing. Move it at the reset.
  • One flex day a week. Picked at the reset, before the week starts. It excuses everything except the never-skip items. Days 29 and 30 have no flex day — finish clean.
  • Miss once, fine. Never miss twice. Habits die on the second day, not the first.
  • Only change the plan at the reset. Sunday changes are maintenance. Wednesday changes are quitting slowly.
  • Give every daily item a time, glued to something that already happens — coffee, the commute, dinner. Hardest thing in your best hour; anything physical before dinner.
The weekly reset

Twenty minutes, same time every week — in person if you're together, a call if you're not. Put it on the weekly list; it's the first thing to disappear if you don't.

Answer the four questions on the card, pick next week's flex day, and move or cut anything that kept getting missed. Then download a backup.

Don't run it late. Todoist's free plan only remembers one week of completions — a reset that's eight days late means last week is gone.

How it all fits together

Todoist owns the day. It reminds you, you tick it. That's the whole daily routine. Shared daily things appear on both your lists — you each tick your own, and that's how the tracker knows you both showed up.

The tracker owns the month. The grid, the sun, the targets. It updates itself every night from Todoist.

This page owns the reset. Once a week, twenty minutes. Nothing here updates on its own — after a reset, fix Todoist if the plan changed and download a backup.

And day 31: in week four, pick the two or three things that clearly paid off and keep them for good. Drop the rest, no guilt. Three habits that survive is a win.
